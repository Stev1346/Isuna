# User Personas and Jobs to Be Done

> **Classification:** Internal  
> **Authority:** Research synthesis; validate with users  
> **Audience:** Founder and Cursor  
> **Status:** Founding MVP baseline  
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-16


## How to use these personas

These are behavioural planning tools, not demographic stereotypes. A real household may match several. Validate assumptions through interviews and product use; do not encode inferred income, relationship status, gender, culture, or financial sophistication into eligibility or scoring.

## Shared jobs across personas

- Establish an accurate and sufficiently current household picture.
- Reduce the effort of monthly maintenance.
- Understand changes without needing accounting expertise.
- Make decisions with another person without blame or ambiguity.
- Preserve closed history and retrieve evidence later.
- Retain ownership of data through export and deletion controls.

## The Organizer — individual

- **Context:** A professional using several accounts and a spreadsheet irregularly.
- **Challenges:** Information is scattered; irregular expenses are forgotten; no dependable monthly record.
- **Current tools:** Bank apps, notes, spreadsheet, tax folders.
- **Frustrations:** Repeated entry, dashboards without context, difficulty knowing whether balances are current.
- **Goals:** Build one accurate picture and a sustainable monthly routine.

### Jobs to be done

- When I begin a month, help me see what is current and what needs updating.
- When I review spending, help me distinguish a one-off issue from a pattern.
- When I close a month, preserve what I knew and decided at that time.

- **Reasons to adopt:** A guided system that is more structured than a spreadsheet but still transparent.
- **Reasons to leave:** Too much setup, unexplained calculations, difficult exports or an app that feels judgemental.
- **Onboarding needs:** Progressive onboarding, sample categories, clear “why we ask,” ability to pause and resume.
- **Most important features:** Accounts, imports, reconciliation, dashboard, meeting, snapshots.
## The Coordinating Couple

- **Context:** Two partners with a mix of shared and separate accounts and different levels of financial confidence.
- **Challenges:** They discuss money reactively, duplicate work, and lack a shared source of truth.
- **Current tools:** Shared spreadsheet, messaging, individual bank apps.
- **Frustrations:** Permission ambiguity, one partner carrying the mental load, meetings dominated by transaction details.
- **Goals:** Create a shared routine with clear responsibilities, decisions and follow-up.

### Jobs to be done

- When one of us updates data, help both of us understand the impact.
- When we meet, guide us through facts before decisions.
- When we assign an action, keep ownership and due date visible.

- **Reasons to adopt:** True household membership, calm meetings and a shared historical record.
- **Reasons to leave:** One partner cannot understand the product, invitations fail, or one member can perform unsafe owner actions.
- **Onboarding needs:** Invite flow, role explanation, shared setup checklist, accessible language and non-blaming variance copy.
- **Most important features:** Households, roles, meetings, action items, dashboard, reports.
## The Debt-Recovery Household

- **Context:** A household carrying credit cards, loans or a vehicle loan while cash flow is tight.
- **Challenges:** Minimum payments obscure progress; interest costs are discouraging; priorities change without a plan.
- **Current tools:** Lender portals, calculators, debt snowball spreadsheet.
- **Frustrations:** Conflicting balances, payoff dates that silently assume unrealistic payments, shame-inducing UI.
- **Goals:** Understand balances, choose a repayment order, track real progress and protect a starter emergency fund.

### Jobs to be done

- When I add debts, show the effect of minimum and extra payments without guaranteeing outcomes.
- When balances change, update estimates using explicit assumptions.
- When progress is slow, show factual movement without gamification.

- **Reasons to adopt:** One place for budget, debt, cash flow and meeting decisions.
- **Reasons to leave:** Misleading payoff estimates, moralizing language, or inability to correct balances.
- **Onboarding needs:** Plain-language assumptions, optional strategy education, privacy reassurance, a quick first win.
- **Most important features:** Debt tracking, payment history, budget, emergency goal, dashboard.
## The Wealth-Building Household

- **Context:** Income is stable and the household is balancing mortgage, registered accounts, education savings and medium-term goals.
- **Challenges:** Assets are spread across institutions; contribution decisions are disconnected from cash flow.
- **Current tools:** Broker portals, bank apps, annual adviser statement, spreadsheet.
- **Frustrations:** No unified net-worth history; contribution rates are difficult to compare; projections feel opaque.
- **Goals:** Coordinate cash, goals and contributions while keeping a reliable long-term record.

### Jobs to be done

- When we invest, show contributions separately from market-value changes where data allows.
- When we review the quarter, help us identify trends and trade-offs.
- When assumptions are missing, say so rather than inventing precision.

- **Reasons to adopt:** A household-level view and structured quarterly review.
- **Reasons to leave:** Insufficient account types, opaque score, weak history or advice-like recommendations.
- **Onboarding needs:** Registered account labels, manual balance entry, contribution import, clear disclaimers.
- **Most important features:** Investments, net worth, quarterly review, snapshots, goals.
## The FI Planner

- **Context:** A financially engaged household estimating whether its savings and investing path could support future independence.
- **Challenges:** Different calculators use different assumptions; current behaviour is disconnected from projections.
- **Current tools:** FI calculators, spreadsheets, community rules of thumb.
- **Frustrations:** False certainty, no link to verified household data, difficulty revisiting assumptions.
- **Goals:** Use their actual financial record to explore—not predict—possible paths.

### Jobs to be done

- When I set assumptions, show their effect and retain the assumptions with the result.
- When data is incomplete, show a range or no result.
- When I revisit a projection, distinguish current data from the historical scenario.

- **Reasons to adopt:** Transparent scenarios grounded in the household record.
- **Reasons to leave:** Personalized advice, hidden assumptions, market promises or complex scenario tooling too early.
- **Onboarding needs:** Explicit educational framing, editable assumptions and a simple scenario before advanced planning.
- **Most important features:** Investment tracking, savings/investing rate, FI inputs, disclosures, historical trends.


## Adoption journey

| Stage | User question | Isuna response |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluate | “Is this safe and worth the setup?” | Plain-language scope, privacy, sample workflow and no exaggerated claims |
| Set up | “What information do I need?” | Resumable checklist, progressive disclosure and import options |
| Trust | “Are these numbers right?” | Data freshness, reconciliation and traceable calculations |
| Meet | “What should we discuss?” | Structured meeting that separates facts, reflection and decisions |
| Close | “Will this month stay accurate?” | Validation, exceptions, immutable snapshot and report |
| Continue | “Is the routine helping?” | Trends, carried actions, quarterly review and reduced setup effort |

## Research priorities

Before paid launch, interview at least a small, diverse sample across these behaviours. Test: willingness to perform manual reconciliation, acceptable onboarding effort, meeting language, household permission expectations, perceived value of immutable snapshots, CSV pain points, and reaction to the health score.
