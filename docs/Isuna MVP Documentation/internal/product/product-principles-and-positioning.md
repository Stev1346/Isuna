# Product Principles and Positioning

> **Classification:** Internal  
> **Authority:** Positioning and experience authority  
> **Audience:** Founder and Cursor  
> **Status:** Founding MVP baseline  
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-16


## Product principles

1. **Clarity before cleverness.** Every number should have a plain-language definition and a path to detail.
2. **Trust is earned through evidence.** Show freshness, reconciliation, assumptions, audit history and limitations.
3. **The household owns the data.** Make export and deletion understandable; avoid hostage-style retention.
4. **Decisions matter more than dashboards.** Summaries should lead to attention, discussion or action.
5. **Closed history is a record.** Never silently rewrite a completed month.
6. **Collaboration needs explicit permissions.** Shared access is not a shared password.
7. **Financial stress deserves respectful design.** Use neutral language; no shame, celebration confetti or manipulative streaks.
8. **Progressive depth.** Make the next useful step obvious while allowing supporting detail.
9. **Safe defaults, visible exceptions.** Protect users without hiding why an action is blocked.
10. **Build the smallest complete loop.** Prefer a reliable end-to-end cycle over broad partial features.
11. **Accessibility is ordinary quality.** Keyboard, screen-reader, contrast and reduced-motion support are acceptance criteria.
12. **Teach the founder.** Code and architecture must remain understandable to the sole developer.

## Positioning statement

For Canadian individuals, couples, and households who want more than transaction tracking, **Isuna is a Family Financial Operating System** that connects budgeting, reconciliation, goals, net worth and structured household reviews. Unlike single-purpose budgeting apps or fragile spreadsheets, Isuna turns financial data into a repeatable decision routine and preserves each closed period as a dependable historical record.

## Competitive category

Primary category: **household financial management software**.  
Distinctive category language: **Family Financial Operating System**.

Use the distinctive phrase to explain the integrated routine, not to claim market leadership or a regulated status.

## What Isuna is

- a secure household workspace;
- an organizational, planning and reporting tool;
- a monthly and quarterly review system;
- a transparent record of user-entered or imported information;
- a collaborative source of truth;
- a platform designed to expand into useful automation.

## What Isuna is not

- a bank, payment service, lender, brokerage or custodian;
- a credit bureau or credit score;
- an accountant, financial planner, tax professional, lawyer or mortgage adviser;
- a promise of investment, debt-payoff or retirement results;
- a substitute for source statements or professional advice;
- a tool for hiding financial activity from authorized household members.

## Voice and tone

| Quality | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | “Two accounts still need reconciliation.” | “Warning! Your finances are out of control.” |
| Direct | “Closing saves a permanent record.” | Dense financial jargon |
| Respectful | “Spending was above plan.” | “You failed your budget.” |
| Transparent | “Estimate assumes the current payment continues.” | Implied certainty |
| Collaborative | “Review together” | Assuming every user has a partner |
| Helpful | Explain the next action | Excessive motivational slogans |

## Approved descriptions

### Short

Isuna is a secure Family Financial Operating System that helps households understand their full financial picture, review progress, make decisions and preserve reliable monthly history.

### Medium

Isuna brings budgeting, accounts, debt, savings, investments, net worth and household reviews into one calm workspace. Reconciliation and data-freshness cues help users understand what they can trust. A guided Monthly Money Meeting turns the numbers into decisions, action items and an immutable month-end snapshot.

### Elevator pitch

Most financial tools capture transactions but leave households to assemble the bigger picture and decide what to do next. Isuna creates a repeatable operating rhythm: set up the household, maintain accurate data, review the month together, record decisions and close a permanent snapshot. It is Canadian-first, collaborative, transparent about assumptions and designed to grow from manual control into responsible automation.

## Feature naming principles

- Name the user outcome, not the implementation: **Monthly Money Meeting**, not `period_workflow`.
- Use familiar Canadian terms while storing neutral domain codes.
- Reserve “closed” for immutable reporting state.
- Use “archive” for hiding inactive operational records without deleting history.
- Use “remove” for membership changes and “delete” only for destructive data operations.
- Use “estimate,” “scenario,” or “projection” when results depend on assumptions.
- Avoid “smart,” “AI,” “guaranteed,” “optimal,” “safe,” or “secure” unless the exact meaning is explained and supportable.
