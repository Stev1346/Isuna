# Product Overview

> **Visibility:** Public  
> **Status:** Founding MVP documentation; capabilities may not yet be available  
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-16


## Product promise

> **See your whole financial picture, know what needs attention, and decide what comes next—together.**

## The main experience

Isuna is designed around a repeatable household routine rather than a collection of disconnected calculators:

1. Set up the household and its financial accounts.
2. Add or import monthly transactions.
3. Review categories, budget performance and account balances.
4. Reconcile accounts so the household understands what is current.
5. Review the month through a guided Monthly Money Meeting.
6. Record decisions, action items and next-month goals.
7. Close the month and preserve a permanent snapshot and report.
8. Use closed months to understand trends in a quarterly review.

## Major planned MVP features

### Household workspace

Create a household, invite a partner, use clear Owner and Editor roles, and switch securely if a user belongs to more than one household.

### Complete financial picture

Track chequing, savings, credit cards, lines of credit, loans, mortgages, property and Canadian account types such as TFSA, RRSP, FHSA and RESP. The MVP begins with manual values and CSV imports rather than direct bank connections.

### Budgeting and transaction review

Plan income and spending by month, categorize transactions, split purchases, identify transfers and compare actual results with the plan.

### Reconciliation and freshness

Record statement balances and dates, identify differences and see which accounts or values need attention before the month is closed.

### Debt, savings and investments

Track debt progress, savings goals, emergency-fund information and manual investment contributions and balances. Calculations and scenarios will state their assumptions and limitations.

### Executive dashboard

See a small number of useful indicators, previous-period comparisons, progress, data status and the next actions that matter. The dashboard is intended to avoid excessive charts and spreadsheet-like density.

## Monthly Money Meetings

The Monthly Money Meeting is the centre of the planned experience. It guides a household through income, spending, budget differences, debt, savings, investments, net-worth change, wins, challenges, upcoming expenses, decisions, action items and goals. It is intended to make financial conversations calmer and more structured.

## Quarterly reviews

After three months have been closed, Isuna is planned to summarize the quarter from those permanent records. The household can review trends, progress, decisions and priorities without silently recalculating old months from changing live data.

## Why Isuna is different

Many budgeting tools focus on transaction capture. Isuna is designed to connect **data quality, household review, decisions, follow-through and reliable history**. A closed month is intended to remain an accurate record of what was known and decided at that time. Corrections create a new transparent version rather than erasing the previous record.

## Canadian-first focus

The initial design uses Canadian terminology, CAD, provinces and territories, registered account types and Canadian privacy expectations. The underlying architecture is intended to support future countries and currencies without claiming that international support already exists.

## Long-term direction

Potential later phases include better automation, connected accounts, advanced planning, mobile capabilities, professional collaboration, international support and privacy-conscious assistance. These are directional ideas, not release commitments.

## Important limitation

Isuna helps users organize and understand information they enter or connect. It is not a financial institution, adviser, tax preparer, accountant, lawyer, lender, credit bureau or brokerage.
